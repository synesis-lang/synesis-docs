1 Synesis: Structuring Knowledge for Data-Driven Decisions

A guide for decision makers and knowledge professionals

1.1 The Challenge of the Data Era

We live in an era summed up by the acronym VUCA: Volatility, Uncertainty, Complexity and Ambiguity. In this context, making effective decisions takes more than intuition — it requires integrating and analysing multiple data sources, identifying not just correlations but cause-and-effect relations among the observed phenomena.

As recent research on Causal Knowledge Graphs highlights, mere correlation between data can lead to wrong conclusions. The classic example: there is a statistical correlation between chocolate consumption and the number of Nobel laureates across countries — but it would be absurd to conclude that handing out chocolate will increase an organization’s scientific output.

The real challenge is capturing causal relations, not just superficial correlations.

1.2 Where Does the Synesis Language Come In?

Synesis is a structured language for the systematic capture of causal knowledge from documentary sources and qualitative observations.

While automated systems such as statistical causal discovery work on numerical data, much organizational knowledge is scattered across:

  • Reports and technical documents
  • Interviews and qualitative surveys
  • Specialized literature and manuals
  • Incident records and lessons learned
  • Engagement and organizational climate surveys

Synesis offers a standard way to extract, document and structure causal relations from these textual sources, making them processable and integrable with other analysis systems.

1.3 The Problem Synesis Addresses

1.3.1 Limitation 1: Qualitative knowledge is not quantified

When a causal relation is extracted from a document (for instance, “lack of training causes operating errors”), it is purely qualitative. There is no way to predict the probability or magnitude of the effect.

How Synesis helps: by structuring these relations systematically, they can later be integrated with numerical data to quantify causal effects.

1.3.2 Limitation 2: Statistical discovery can invert cause and effect

Statistical causal discovery algorithms applied to biased or incomplete data may derive relations whose causal direction is inverted.

How Synesis helps: knowledge documented in Synesis works as prior knowledge to validate or correct statistical findings.

1.3.3 Limitation 3: Data from different contexts do not talk to each other

Traditional causal discovery works on a single dataset. It cannot combine observations from different departments, periods or conditions.

How Synesis helps: the standard structure allows knowledge from several sources to be integrated in a common vocabulary.

1.4 Practical Applications

1.4.1 Human Resources

Scenario: HR needs to recommend initiatives to improve engagement, but each department has different characteristics.

With Synesis:

  • Document causal relations from the organizational behaviour literature
  • Record engagement survey observations with structured interpretations
  • Identify which factors (career opportunities, clarity of vision, teamwork) cause which outcomes (motivation, satisfaction, retention)
  • Recommend initiatives based on documented evidence
SOURCE @engagement_survey_q4_2024_sales
    access_date: 2024-12-15
END SOURCE

ITEM @engagement_survey_q4_2024_sales
    quote: "I feel there are no growth opportunities for me here"
    memo: The employee expresses frustration with limited career paths and links the lack of progression to lower motivation.
    chain: lack_of_career_opportunities -> INHIBITS -> motivation
END ITEM

1.4.2 Failure and Incident Analysis

Scenario: an operations team needs to find root causes of failures in complex systems.

With Synesis:

  • Extract causal relations from technical manuals and troubleshooting guides
  • Document each incident with the causal chain identified
  • Build a knowledge base that speeds up future diagnoses
SOURCE @incident_report_2024_0892
    access_date: 2024-11-10
END SOURCE

ITEM @incident_report_2024_0892
    quote: "Intermittent disconnection during file downloads"
    memo: Pattern consistent with a MAC configuration error; reproducible under the same network conditions.
    chain: mac_configuration_error -> INHIBITS -> stable_connectivity
END ITEM

1.4.3 Market Analysis and Negotiations

Scenario: a sales team wants to understand why some negotiations succeed and others do not.

With Synesis:

  • Record observations from each negotiation (meetings, objections, decisions)
  • Identify causal patterns between actions and results
  • Combine with engagement data to understand how internal factors affect external performance
SOURCE @negotiation_review_2024_156
    access_date: 2024-10-22
END SOURCE

ITEM @negotiation_review_2024_156
    quote: "The client mentioned trust in the technical team as the deciding factor"
    memo: Showing technical competence during the proposal shaped perceived trust, which in turn enabled the deal.
    chain: technical_competence -> FAVORS -> client_trust -> FAVORS -> deal_closing
END ITEM

1.5 How It Works in Practice

1.5.1 1. You document the source

SOURCE @engagement_survey_2024
    access_date: 2024-12-15
END SOURCE

All knowledge is traceable to its origin — a document, an interview, a report.

1.5.2 2. You record observations and interpretations

ITEM @engagement_survey_2024
    quote: "I don't know where the company is heading"
    memo: The employee expresses a lack of clarity about strategic direction
    chain: lack_of_strategic_clarity -> INHIBITS -> engagement
END ITEM

Each item connects textual evidence with your interpretation and the causal relation identified.

1.5.3 3. You define your conceptual vocabulary

ONTOLOGY engagement
    description: The employee's level of commitment and emotional connection with the organization.
    group: organizational_outcomes
END ONTOLOGY

ONTOLOGY lack_of_strategic_clarity
    description: Absence of clear communication about the organization's direction, goals or vision.
    group: organizational_factors
END ONTOLOGY

Concepts are defined explicitly, keeping the project consistent over time.

1.5.4 4. The system validates and structures

The Synesis compiler:

  • Checks annotations against the defined model (the template)
  • Produces structured formats (JSON, CSV, spreadsheets)
  • Enables integration with analysis systems and graph databases

Field names and allowed relations are defined by the organization itself. To get started with an assistant, see the Synesis Skill for Claude Desktop; to code large volumes with AI and human review, synesis-coder.

1.6 Benefits for the Organization

1.6.1 Full traceability

Every conclusion can be traced to the original evidence. When someone asks “why do you recommend this?”, the answer is documented.

1.6.2 Preserved institutional knowledge

Specialist knowledge, which usually exists only in people’s heads, is recorded and available to the organization.

1.6.3 Qualitative–quantitative integration

Causal relations documented qualitatively can be combined with statistical analyses for cross-validation.

1.6.4 A foundation for artificial intelligence

Organizations investing in Causal Knowledge Graphs need structured knowledge as input. Synesis provides that structure from documentary sources.

1.6.5 Grounded decision-making

Instead of decisions based on guesswork or superficial correlations, the organization works with documented, verifiable causal relations.

1.7 Comparison with Other Approaches

Approach Data source Type of relation Limitation
Traditional statistics Numerical Correlation Does not tell cause from effect
Statistical causal discovery Numerical Causal (estimated) Needs large volumes; may get direction wrong
Traditional qualitative research Textual Qualitative Unstructured; hard to integrate
Synesis Textual Causal (documented) Needs human analysis; complements statistics

The ideal combination: Synesis to structure qualitative knowledge, statistical causal discovery for numerical data, and both integrated into a Causal Knowledge Graph.

1.8 An Integrated Scenario

Imagine a company that wants to improve at the same time:

  • The success rate of sales negotiations
  • Employee engagement
  • Project delivery quality

Flow with Synesis:

  1. Structured collection
    • Engagement surveys documented in Synesis
    • Negotiation reviews documented in Synesis
    • Quality reports documented in Synesis
  2. Finding connections
    • Statistical discovery identifies: Engagement → Delivery Quality
    • Synesis documents: Teamwork → Engagement (from the literature)
    • Synesis documents: Delivery Quality → Client Trust (from negotiations)
  3. Integrated graph
    • Teamwork → Engagement → Quality → Trust → Negotiation Success
  4. Grounded recommendation
    • Investing in teamwork initiatives has a documented cascade effect up to commercial results

1.9 Getting Started

1.9.1 For Organizations

  1. Pick a pilot domain — HR, operations or sales are good starting points
  2. Define the initial vocabulary — which concepts matter for your decisions?
  3. Train the analysts — the syntax is readable; the main effort is defining concepts well
  4. Integrate with existing processes — Synesis complements current tools, it does not replace them
  5. Protect data — remove personal data before using AI services

1.9.2 For Individual Professionals

  1. Try a small project — a literature review, a set of interviews
  2. Focus on causal relations — always ask “what causes what?”
  3. Stay consistent — use the same terms for the same concepts
  4. Document your interpretations — the annotation matters as much as the quotation

1.10 Frequently Asked Questions

1.10.1 Does Synesis replace BI or analytics tools?

No. Synesis works with qualitative knowledge and integrates with quantitative tools. It is a complement, not a substitute.

1.10.2 Do I need technical knowledge to use it?

The syntax is simple and readable. Research, analysis and management professionals can learn it quickly. No programming is required.

1.10.3 How does Synesis relate to artificial intelligence?

AI systems that reason about causes need structured knowledge. Synesis helps build it from textual sources, with a trail that lets people check what the AI or the analyst recorded.

1.10.4 How is Synesis different from simply using spreadsheets?

Spreadsheets have no consistency validation, do not structure causal relations natively and do not guarantee traceability. Synesis was designed for capturing causal knowledge.

1.11 Conclusion

In a world where decisions need to rest increasingly on data, qualitative knowledge cannot be left out. Synesis offers a bridge between expert knowledge — scattered across documents, reports and observations — and the analysis systems that support decisions.

Combining structured causal knowledge with statistical analysis is the next step in organizational intelligence: decisions based not only on data, but on a causal understanding of phenomena.

Synesis: turning scattered knowledge into actionable intelligence.
