Synesis for Legal Professionals

How a structured language turns legal knowledge into auditable institutional heritage

Human knowledge is naturally intricate, full of nuance and deep connections. In the course of their work, legal professionals accumulate contract analyses, read and annotated opinions, case law, meeting notes and hearing records — plus a vast amount of qualitative knowledge.

1 A Language That Both People and Computers Understand

Legal professionals already live with specialized languages. Legal Latin is the clearest example: expressions absent from everyday speech, with a rich vocabulary and their own rules of use, which any jurist understands precisely.

Synesis is a computer Domain-Specific Language (DSL) that plays an analogous role for textual knowledge. Its grammar is recognized by both people and computers. It imposes structure on annotations so that the computer can check automatically whether they conform to a pre-established contract — the template.

The template is flexible enough to serve completely different configurations, defined according to each professional’s or team’s needs. A lawyer may create a configuration to organize cases and documents, classify them into categories and establish explicit connections among them. In a case database, for instance, it is possible to identify themes, recurring concepts and relations between the parties involved.

Synesis records not only what — a fact, an argument, a doctrinal position — but also why and how it connects to other things. A legal professional may document, for example, that the absence of objective good faith in a contractual relationship tends to compromise performance — and link that interpretation directly to the contract passage that supports it, the scholarly article that informed it and the precedent that confirms it. This web of causal and conceptual relations, previously scattered across documents and memory, comes to exist explicitly, verifiably and shareably.

2 The Synesis Ecosystem

The language itself is only the starting point. The Synesis ecosystem is the set of tools that makes the process practical, covering several stages of knowledge governance.

Creating and validating annotations. A dedicated editing environment assists the professional while annotating: it checks in real time whether required fields are filled in, whether values are correct and whether references between documents are consistent. Problems are flagged immediately, before they pile up.

Organizing and exporting knowledge. Accumulated knowledge can be converted into the formats most used in professional data work — spreadsheets, specialized structured formats — and becomes available for analysis, sharing or integration with other tools. For larger projects, the whole network of concepts and relations built along the way can be visualized.

Natural language querying. The compiled collection becomes queryable as a conversation. A professional can ask which decisions deal with a theme, which concepts connect to a specific precedent or how an understanding evolved over time — and obtain answers traceable to the original sources.

3 What Synesis Is Not

It is also important to say what Synesis is not. A compiler validates structure — it ensures that required fields are present and declared values are valid. The intellectual content — interpreting a ruling, identifying a ratio decidendi, building a line of argument — is and remains human work.

Annotations are stored as plain text files, readable in any editor, with no dependence on a platform or proprietary format. Annotations may nonetheless be produced with the help of Artificial Intelligence, speeding up knowledge curation in a process that people can easily audit.

For a law firm, this means that the knowledge built by an experienced partner over decades is not trapped in their memory or lost in disorganized files. It becomes institutional heritage, auditable and evolving.

4 Practical Examples

The examples use Brazilian law and keep the original Portuguese legal terms where they are part of the data.

4.1 The Structure of an Annotation

A Synesis annotation consists of three blocks which together capture all the knowledge produced about a document. The following example starts from a ruling of the Brazilian Superior Court of Justice (STJ) on the right to privacy.

SOURCE block — the traceable origin

Records the source document with all its metadata. No ITEM exists without a corresponding SOURCE — traceability is structurally enforced.

SOURCE @stj_petition_1234
    access_date: 2026-02-17
    document_type: RULING
    court: STJ
    case_number: 1234/2025
    reporting_judge: Justice John Doe
    judgment_date: 2025-11-10
    publication_date: 2025-11-20
    judging_body: Third Panel
    outcome: GRANTED
    url: https://processo.stj.jus.br/processo/pesquisa/?tipoPesquisa=tipoPesquisaNumeroRegistro&termo=1234
END SOURCE

ITEM block — the passage and its interpretation

Records the relevant passage, the professional’s interpretation and the connections with other concepts. The chain field expresses explicit relations between concepts — not only that two themes appear in the same document, but how one prevails over the other.

ITEM @stj_petition_1234
    locus: Federal Constitution, art. 5, item X
    quote: "intimacy, private life, honor and image of persons are inviolable"
    note: The provision enshrines privacy as an absolute fundamental right, the ratio decidendi of the appealed decision.
    chain: Right_to_Privacy -> OVERRULES -> Unauthorized_Exposure
END ITEM

ONTOLOGY block — the controlled vocabulary

Defines the concepts used in annotations. It ensures that every team member uses the same terms with the same meaning — removing fragmentation by synonymy at the root.

ONTOLOGY Right_to_Privacy
    topic: Personality_Rights
    description: Fundamental individual right securing control over one's intimate sphere, personal data and private life, barring unconsented interference by third parties or the State. Basis: Federal Constitution, art. 5, item X; Civil Code, art. 21; General Data Protection Law, art. 2, I.
END ONTOLOGY

4.2 The Template — the Contract Governing the Whole Analysis

The template defines which fields are required, which are optional and which values are accepted. It is what lets the compiler validate each annotation automatically. Although its syntax looks technical, a template is usually defined once at the start of a project — with specialist support or with the help of artificial intelligence.

TEMPLATE privacy_case_law version: "1.0"

SOURCE FIELDS
    REQUIRED document_type, court, case_number
    REQUIRED judgment_date, outcome
    OPTIONAL reporting_judge, judging_body, publication_date
    OPTIONAL url, access_date
END SOURCE FIELDS

ITEM FIELDS
    REQUIRED locus, quote, note
    OPTIONAL chain
END ITEM FIELDS

ONTOLOGY FIELDS
    REQUIRED description
    OPTIONAL topic
END ONTOLOGY FIELDS

FIELD document_type TYPE ENUMERATED
    SCOPE SOURCE
    DESCRIPTION Type of legal document
    VALUES
        RULING:    Appellate ruling
        JUDGMENT:  First-instance judgment
        OPINION:   Legal opinion
        STATUTE:   Normative text
    END VALUES
END FIELD

FIELD outcome TYPE ENUMERATED
    SCOPE SOURCE
    DESCRIPTION Outcome of the judgment
    VALUES
        GRANTED:        Appeal granted
        DENIED:         Appeal denied
        PARTIAL:        Partially granted
        NOT_ADMITTED:   Appeal not admitted
    END VALUES
END FIELD

FIELD court TYPE TEXT
    SCOPE SOURCE
    DESCRIPTION Court or body issuing the document
END FIELD

FIELD case_number TYPE TEXT
    SCOPE SOURCE
    DESCRIPTION Case identification number
END FIELD

FIELD judgment_date TYPE DATE
    SCOPE SOURCE
    DESCRIPTION Date of judgment
END FIELD

FIELD reporting_judge TYPE TEXT
    SCOPE SOURCE
    DESCRIPTION Name of the reporting justice or judge
END FIELD

FIELD judging_body TYPE TEXT
    SCOPE SOURCE
    DESCRIPTION Panel, chamber or body responsible for the judgment
END FIELD

FIELD publication_date TYPE DATE
    SCOPE SOURCE
    DESCRIPTION Date the decision was published
END FIELD

FIELD url TYPE TEXT
    SCOPE SOURCE
    DESCRIPTION Address of the document in the court system
END FIELD

FIELD access_date TYPE DATE
    SCOPE SOURCE
    DESCRIPTION Date the document was accessed
END FIELD

FIELD locus TYPE TEXT
    SCOPE ITEM
    DESCRIPTION Location of the passage within the norm or decision
END FIELD

FIELD quote TYPE QUOTATION
    SCOPE ITEM
    DESCRIPTION Passage quoted literally from the document
END FIELD

FIELD note TYPE MEMO
    SCOPE ITEM
    DESCRIPTION Professional's interpretation and analysis of the passage
END FIELD

FIELD chain TYPE CHAIN
    SCOPE ITEM
    ARITY >= 2
    DESCRIPTION Relation between legal concepts identified in the passage
    RELATIONS
        OVERRULES:        Thesis prevails over a previous understanding
        CITES_PRECEDENT:  Decision grounded in a previous case
        DISTINGUISHES:    Current case distinguished from the general rule
    END RELATIONS
END FIELD

FIELD description TYPE TEXT
    SCOPE ONTOLOGY
    DESCRIPTION Definition of the legal concept, including normative basis
END FIELD

FIELD topic TYPE TOPIC
    SCOPE ONTOLOGY
    DESCRIPTION Branch or legal domain the concept belongs to
END FIELD

This is only one of countless possible configurations. A labour lawyer may structure a case-law base completely differently. A corporate legal department may create one template for contract management, another for regulatory monitoring and another for internal opinions. A legal scholar may map the doctrinal evolution of a concept over decades.

The grammar is the same. What changes is what each professional decides to build within it.
