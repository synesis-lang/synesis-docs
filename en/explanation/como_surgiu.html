1 How Did Synesis Come About?

The original problem

Synesis arose from a concrete study: research on the social acceptance of offshore energy technologies, carried out within OTIC (Offshore Technology Innovation Centre) at the University of São Paulo (USP). The goal was to identify and organize the factors that shape public perception of technological innovations in the context of the energy transition — a problem at once empirical and interpretive.

Addressing it required an integrated workflow combining bibliometric techniques, qualitative coding and artificial intelligence. Tools such as RStudio, the Bibliometrix package and VOSviewer were used to identify the most relevant scientific articles by citation count, exported from databases such as Web of Science and Scopus. Zotero centralized reference management and also served as the initial qualitative annotation environment.

1.1 Zotero as the Starting Point

Zotero was not designed for qualitative analysis. Even so, it proved useful for a first phase of open coding: PDFs could be annotated with highlights and comments, tags attached to passages, and the annotations exported in a custom format.

For that, Zotero was configured with custom templates in its advanced preferences. Each exported annotation followed a structure with specific delimiters:

[-begin_header-]
referencia_bibtex: burke2018
descricao: Explores the relationship between renewable energy systems and political power distribution, examining energy democracy as a framework for energy transitions.
modelo_epistemico: Energy democracy, political power theory
metodo: Conceptual review
[-end_header-]

[-begin-][@burke2018@][!Distributed energy-politics posits that distributed energy sources and technologies enable and organize distributed political power and vice versa.!][%Distributed energy systems can reorganize political power structures%][#Energy System#][&correlates-with&][#Governance#][-end-]

The format worked for small, manually reviewed volumes. But it already carried a structural weakness: the delimiters were textual conventions, not a formal grammar. And the human researcher depended on the same informal format to record interpretations.

1.2 AI Enters the Flow

As the corpus grew — hundreds of abstracts exported as BibTeX and converted to CSV by the bib2csv.py script — processing everything by hand became unfeasible. The solution was to bring in language models through an API to automate the extraction of factors and relations.

The abstract_processor9.py script sent each abstract to the model, with a prompt structured in five parts (system_prompt, task, instructions, relation_types, critical_reminders), and received back the same tag format already used in Zotero. Scale grew. So did the problem.

Language models produce probabilistic text. Regular expressions assume stable patterns. Small variations in the output — an extra space, a badly closed tag, a relation chain out of the expected order — broke parsing. Each adjustment required new regular expressions. The system depended on an ever more fragile balance between model stability, format discipline and the growing complexity of annotations.

1.3 The Proliferation of Patches

To work around the limitations, the toolkit kept growing. editor.py appeared as a visual interface to review and fix the .txt files generated by AI by hand — with right-click support to edit factors, relations and chains. compare_annotations.py was created to measure the distance between what the AI produced and what the researcher meant, using Levenshtein similarity, word overlap and block counts:

INFO: burke2018:
INFO:   Exact Match: 60.0%
INFO:   Levenshtein Similarity: 99.8%
INFO:   Word Overlap: 98.2%
INFO:   Blocks: File1=5, File2=4

docs2txt.py widened the scope beyond scientific abstracts: PDFs, Word documents and HTML pages could now be converted and processed by the same flow — interviews, technical reports, web content. An interactive menu, menu_txt.py, was created just to coordinate the order of the other scripts.

When it became necessary to build a tool only to measure the distance between the AI output and the researcher’s intention, the diagnosis was undeniable: the problem was not technical. It was structural.

1.4 The Insufficiency of the Relational Model

As the analysis advanced, the data stopped being tabular records. They came to include semantic chains:

A → influences → B → enables → C

Representing them in a relational database became artificial. Moving to a graph database was natural. But even with suitable storage the problem persisted: the input was still fragile text, not formal structure. The graph was robust; the language feeding it was not.

1.5 The Tension Between Automation and Interpretation

AI-generated analysis was structured but rigid. Human annotation was flexible but informal. New fields had to be added, interpretations recorded, codes revised, chains expanded, the ontology evolved. The tag-based system made that evolution hard: every structural change required modifying parsing, storage and often the prompt.

The problem became clear: how to integrate structured analysis generated by AI with organic human annotation without sacrificing consistency or flexibility?

1.6 The Insight: Text Needs Grammar

The answer was not a better database, nor more sophisticated regular expressions. What was needed was explicit syntax, formal parsing, semantic validation and a separation between language and configuration.

Hence the idea of turning the annotation format into a language proper, a domain-specific language (DSL):

SOURCE @burke2018
    descricao: Explores the relationship between renewable energy systems and political power distribution, examining energy democracy as a framework for energy transitions.
    modelo_epistemico: Energy democracy, political power theory
    metodo: Conceptual review
END SOURCE

ITEM @burke2018
    text: Distributed energy-politics posits that distributed energy sources and technologies enable and organize distributed political power and vice versa.

    note: Distributed energy systems can reorganize political power structures

    chain: Energy_System -> influences -> Governance
END ITEM

This new approach let human and AI-generated annotation pass through the same text and the same rules, checked by a compiler.

1.7 Synesis as a Synthesis

Synesis is not a middle layer between AI and database.

It is the point where two processes converge: structured, scalable generation by AI, and contextual, evolving human interpretation. Both operate in the same language.

AI can produce annotations in Synesis. The researcher can edit, extend and reinterpret them. The compiler guarantees consistency and traceability.

Structure does not eliminate interpretation. It organizes it.

1.8 The Current Role of Synesis

Synesis answers a contemporary problem: AI models generate structured text, but text is not reliable structure. Without grammar, validation and a controlled ontology, analysis becomes hard to maintain, version and audit.

Synesis formalizes that structure without preventing evolution.

It was born from practical friction — in a real research problem, with real data, in a workflow that grew until its own patches became the obstacle. And it keeps evolving from that same friction.
